# Arc.dev Video Script — Meia Entrada (Student ID Platform)

**Question:** "Tell us about a recent project that you are most proud of and what you've contributed to it."

**Target:** ~90 seconds | **Tone:** Professional, confident, genuine pride

---

**[Opening — ~10s]**

The project I'm most proud of is a student ID platform I built for student organizations across Brazil. It's the system that lets students prove their enrollment so they can get half-price event tickets — what we call "meia entrada" here.

---

**[The Situation — ~20s]**

When I joined, the platform was technically in production — but barely. Every time a new student organization came on board, a developer had to manually configure things. The codebase was a half-finished Next.js and Supabase app that didn't support most of the actual business requirements. And here's the thing — I had *zero* experience with either framework. I had to learn everything on the fly while delivering at speed.

On top of that, the two other developers on the project started taking shortcuts — shipping buggy code, misunderstanding requirements. One of them had even completely disabled the authorization layer, exposing all student data publicly.

---

**[Contributions — ~35s]**

I took the lead. I went back to fundamentals — documented every business requirement, researched competing products, sketched designs in Figma, and translated all of it into a responsive UI that worked from the smallest Android phone all the way to desktop.

The feature I'm most proud of is the configurable form builder. Every student organization has different requirements — some need custom fields, some need rich text instructions, some need image uploads. I designed and built the entire thing — both the student-facing form logic and the admin panel where organizations set it all up themselves. It eliminated the manual developer work completely.

I also reimplemented most of the logic from the other devs, rebuilt the authorization layer properly with Supabase's row-level security, and wrote a full test suite — unit tests, integration tests, and E2E tests with Playwright covering every form scenario and admin flow. I set up the CI/CD pipeline too.

---

**[Outcome — ~20s]**

Today the platform serves over 120 student organizations and processes more than a hundred thousand digital student ID cards every year. It went from a project that needed constant developer babysitting to a self-service platform where organizations can onboard themselves.

What makes me proud isn't just the technical work. It's that I walked into a messy situation with tools I'd never used, teammates who weren't delivering, and a product that was half-broken — and I turned it into something that genuinely works, that's secure, and that thousands of students actually rely on.

---

**[Closing — ~5s]**

That's what I love doing — taking ownership of hard problems and shipping real solutions.

---

**Total: ~90 seconds (conversational speaking pace)**

## Delivery Tips

- Speak naturally — don't read. Use this as a guide, not a teleprompter.
- Pause after key moments: "zero experience with either framework", "exposing all student data publicly", "a hundred thousand digital student ID cards every year"
- The closing line is the takeaway — slow down and deliver it with conviction
- If you go over 90s, the "two other developers" section is the easiest to trim
- Record 2-3 takes and pick the best one
